abstract: "When a clinical LLM correctly diagnoses a common disease, has it reasoned, or has it just sampled from the empirical distribution of medical text? We design diagnostic cases where the correct answer is *not* the most prevalent answer — where constraint satisfaction, causal exclusion, and evidence integration push against the prior. Across frontier LLMs the gap is large and consistent: high accuracy on prevalent presentations, sharp collapse when the prior contradicts the constraints. The work reframes medical LLM evaluation around four operational primitives — constraint satisfaction, causal exclusion, evidence integration, calibration under noisy observations — rather than aggregate accuracy on distribution-matched benchmarks. The implication: published medical-LLM benchmarks may be measuring memorized epidemiology rather than reasoning."
