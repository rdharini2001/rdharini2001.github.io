abstract: "Flow matching, diffusion, and stochastic interpolants all describe the same object — a transport path between noise and data — but they impose very different geometric constraints on that path. We study how the curvature of learned velocity fields, the density concentration along interpolants, and the asymmetry between forward and reverse trajectories actually shape likelihood calibration and sample quality. The motivating observation is that two models with identical FID can have wildly different transport geometries, and the differences predict failure modes the headline metric does not. The work connects to mean-flow-style finite-displacement reformulations and to recent work treating one-step generation as the primary training object rather than a distillation artifact."
