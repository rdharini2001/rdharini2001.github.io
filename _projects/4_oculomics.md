abstract: "Can the retina act as a non-invasive readout of systemic disease? We build statistical-learning and representation-learning frameworks that project ocular structure onto cardiovascular, oncologic, and metabolic risk axes. In a cohort of women with ischemia and no obstructive coronary artery disease, OCTA-derived microvascular remodeling patterns separate coronary microvascular dysfunction from controls — suggesting that capillary-level retinal phenotypes track a shared systemic microvascular state, not just ocular pathology. We extend this to retinal-perivascular geometry for cardio-renal-metabolic risk, deep fundus representations for pan-cancer and cardio-oncology risk, and 3D OCT morphology for treatment-response modeling. The unifying claim: the eye is a low-dimensional, optically accessible window onto systems-level physiology, and learned ocular representations are early, non-invasive biomarkers of disease."
