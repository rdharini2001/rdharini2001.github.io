abstract: "Bone-marrow biopsies in multiple myeloma are usually scored by plasma-cell percentage — a single number summarizing a richly structured tissue ecology. We treat the marrow as a spatial system: plasma cells, immune cells, and stromal compartments laid out in patterns that encode neighborhood geometry, local composition, and immune-plasma interaction. Using spatial tissue modeling and deep learning, we extract architecture-level phenotypes and find that they separate overall survival in ways aggregate cellularity does not — and that the separation is sex-specific, with male and female patients drawing on different architectural axes for prognostic signal. The implication: sex effects in MM survival may be partly mediated by differences in marrow architecture, not just hormonal or genetic factors."
