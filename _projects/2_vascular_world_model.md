I am developing a **vascular world model** where a generative agent learns plausible, physics-consistent vascular states conditioned on tissue context, disease state, and intervention. Rather than treating vascular morphology as static image features, the model treats it as a structured generative object: branching topology, perfusion constraints, and treatment response are modeled as **coupled state transitions**.

This sits at the intersection of generative modeling, world models, and physics-informed learning, and connects to my broader interest in state modeling — learning compact representations of biological systems from partial, noisy observations.

### Themes
- Vascular morphology as a structured generative state.
- Physics constraints (perfusion, branching topology) as priors on transitions.
- Conditioning on disease state and intervention for counterfactual reasoning.
